# Super Champion derailleur catalogues

## Tarif 10 Janvier 1938 — review 2026-11-24

Inspected all six scans of the [tariff and supplement](https://www.disraeligears.co.uk/site/super_champion_-_tarif_10_janvier_1938.html); the cover and supplement state 10 January 1938. Scan 2 covers Course, Compétition, Tour de France and Touriste Standard with service-part lists. The 1938 Compétition set includes a spoke protector and three-sprocket freewheel but explicitly excludes the separately sold chain guide; the 1936 package included it. Touriste Standard is advertised for 12–15 teeth difference. Scan 3 illustrates double-wrap Grand Sport and the offset-pulley Route, plus a cable-operated front changer in steel/aluminium bronze, cadmium/chromed. Scan 4 lists three distinct Rex models (Course, Touriste, Professionnel), with down-tube control, top-tube control and chain-tension adjustment respectively. Three- and four-speed freewheels are offered in multiple combinations, but their unnumbered listings cannot be crosswalked to marked specimens 2251–2253. Fittings, individual spare parts, tools and dropouts are outside the component categories; scans 1/5/6 are cover, dealer terms and publicity.

Applied source 558: extended Compétition 4799 1932–1936 → 1932–1938 with both edition-specific chain-guide arrangements; extended Touriste Standard 12526 1936–1936 → 1936–1938 and added its 12–15T range; enriched Grand Sport 4802 and Route 4807 with the maker drawings and specifications without changing their dates. Photographs of 4802 and 4807 show the corresponding two-pulley and offset-pulley arrangements. Added cable-operated front derailleur 12533, distinct from direct-operated Luxe 2666; created Rex brand 771 and rear-derailleur category association and added Rex Course 12534, Touriste 12535 and Professionnel 12536. Rex is separately marked and priced in the tariff; the catalogue does not imply production endpoints beyond the 1938 attestation. Readback matched the eight changed rows, and a repeat application affected zero rows. Super Champion and Rex near-duplicate reports found zero pairs. All eight changed rows have zero bike-spec links, and no relevant Rex or Super Champion derailleur bike-spec value was found. The generator/live comparison showed zero gained or changed matches and the same 15 pre-existing Huret Rival proposed losses noted below; it changed no links. The quality report found zero errors.

Before images: 4799, source 554, `Duralumin and aluminium bronze derailleur with chrome-plated parts; supplied with three-sprocket freewheel, chain guide and spoke protector.`; 12526, source 554, `Steel and aluminium bronze derailleur with cadmium-plated parts; supplied with three-sprocket freewheel and spoke protector.`; 4802, 1938–1940/source 1, `Super Champion Grand Sport, France`; 4807, 1937–1938/source 1, `Super Champion Route, France`. The scoped Super Champion near-duplicate baseline returned no pairs; before loading, no exact Rex brand or Rex component title existed in the database.

**Course hold:** The Course drawing shows a rounded end in profile, but the small view does not show whether it is a ball or a flattened marked knob. It also depicts a general assembly rather than a uniquely identified specimen. It is not sufficient to attribute 1938 availability to either [4800](https://www.velobase.com/Pages/ViewComponent.aspx?ID=D27C8286-7AB3-4FBD-8F32-46EB62E774B1) or [4801](https://www.velobase.com/Pages/ViewComponent.aspx?ID=1ECCD35D-A5A8-442E-8034-ECBABAC062DE); keep the hold and do not change either date. The available listing is evidence for the Course family only.

## 1947 and 1949 catalogue review — 2026-11-24

Read all four scans of the [October 1947 catalogue](https://www.disraeligears.co.uk/site/super_champion_-_catalogue_1er_octobre_1947.html). Scan 2 offers Tour de France, Mondial and Mondial Luxe, Sport and Sport Luxe. Tour de France is again offered after the wartime interruption and has a striking fork and cable-operated chain detensioner. The steel Sport and light-metal Sport Luxe are listed separately; all models are three-speed, with four-speed narrow chain offered on request for the Luxe models and Tour de France. Scan 3 offers a fixed-position rotating grip control in three-, four- and five-speed versions. Racks, chain guards, pedal protectors and loose fittings are outside component categories; scans 1 and 4 are cover and company history.

Applied 1947 source 556: added rotating grip shifter 12531, distinct from the downtube lever pictured for generic 6321, and enriched 4806 Tour de France. Old 4806: `Super Champion Professional Tour de France, France, 333 grams (Actual)`, 1937–1950, source 1. Its measured 333g and existing year range are preserved. Readback matched both rows; a repeat application affected zero rows. Other rear models lack a sufficiently secure match to a particular existing specimen; no duplicate family rows were added.

Read all eight pages and the separately indexed February 1949 accompanying letter of the [1949 catalogue](https://www.disraeligears.co.uk/site/les_creations_super_champion_-_catalogue_general_1949.html). Scans 2–4 list Mondial/Mondial Luxe, Sport/Sport Luxe, and Tour de France, with individual service part numbers; scan 5 shows the new Luxe direct-operated front derailleur, 120g listed, and double chainrings for three-arm cranks in 47/44, 50/47 and 50/46. The letter explicitly names the front derailleur and double chainrings as new and confirms the six-year Tour de France production interruption and its return by February 1949; the 1947 catalogue documents an earlier offer. Scans 6–8 cover racks, accessories and company history, outside component categories.

Applied 1949 source 557: retitled generic front 2666 to Super Champion Luxe front derailleur, added its catalogue specifications and 1949 attestation, and added double chainrings 12532 with the missing category-brand association. The pictured 2666 specimen has the same direct lever, clamp and Super Champion-marked guide as scan 5. Before image: title `Super Champion`, description `Super Champion, France`, dates NULL/NULL, source 1. The catalogue explicitly calls the design new, but this is not proof of the photographed item's manufacture year. The 47/44 option specifies 2.38mm chain and chrome outer/cadmium inner finish; the source does not explicitly assign those details to the other tooth combinations. Readback matched both rows and a repeat application affected zero rows.

**Sport 46 specimen decision:** Compared the photographs for [4798](https://www.velobase.com/Pages/ViewComponent.aspx?ID=C0C817B7-2324-4A0E-802F-AF0E6E511D87) and [4809](https://www.velobase.com/Pages/ViewComponent.aspx?ID=13AA7379-95B0-42C7-A0A5-ABCA5A6E0B29). The former has a toothed upper guide pulley and is pictured with a Sport 46-labelled box; the latter has a smooth upper guide pulley with large side plates. Keep these photographed configurations separate. This does not prove the pulley was never replaced or establish a maker crosswalk between Sport 46 Dural and 1947/49 Sport Luxe; neither specimen is relabelled. The 1947 and 1949 Sport illustrations depict a family and do not prove which specific specimen they represent. Course 4800/4801 remains unresolved. Brand near-duplicate reports before and after returned zero pairs. The four added or changed records have no bike-spec links; no Super Champion derailleur, shifter or chainring bike-spec values were found to link. No generator references were found for the existing reviewed IDs.

Generator/live-DB comparison after both loads covered 118 catalogues: zero gained or changed matches and 15 proposed losses, all generic Huret Rival links to 4401, outside this brand and consistent with the [existing Huret ambiguity decision](huret.md#novemberdecember-1985-dealer-sheets--applied-and-validated-2026-11-23). No links were modified by the comparison.
