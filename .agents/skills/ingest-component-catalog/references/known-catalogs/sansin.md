## CE-60 and HB-CE60 catalogue comparison — 2026-11-28

[Bianchi's 1990 catalogue](https://www.bikecatalogs.com/download/2797) names a 36-hole Sansin CE-60 set on the Volpe. [Bridgestone's 1991 RB-T table, p. 39](https://ebykr.com/showroom/bridgestone/1991/rb-t/) specifies Sansin CE-60, 36-hole, quick release and a front re-tension guard nut. Neither bike has a live spec for those fitments. Bianchi Volpe's 1990 CSV row is excluded because that year's cross-terrain variant is flat-bar. The separately loaded 1991 Bianchi Volpe specifies AE-60, linked to 9016. The existing five links to 9007 cover four 1987 Miyata specs and the 1989 Bianchi Brava, whose catalogue actually uses the wording SunTour CE-60.

The [1992 Cannondale catalogue, printed p. 20 (PDF p. 19)](https://vintagecannondale.com/year/1992/1992.pdf#page=19), source 563, explicitly names Sansin CE-60 twice for the M400 and describes the hubs as sealed with chrome-moly axles. The same specification lists a separate screw-on freewheel. The [1992 Miyata road specification table](https://www.ragandbone.ca/PDFs/Miyata%20Catalogue%20PDFs/Miyata%20Catalogue%2092.pdf) independently names CE-60 on the 700 GT, using the term freehub for its fitment. Cannondale supplies the 1992 endpoint and the threaded-fitment context. The description for 9007 retains the 1987 maker's CE-60 and CE-60S distinctions and attributes the later sealed CE-60 wording to the M400. The 1993–94 Cannondale scans and 1993 Miyata scan returned no later exact-code attestation in this review.

The [1994 SR SunTour manual](https://www.bikecatalogs.com/download/2243), printed pp. 16–17 and 35, lists HB-CE60-F/R for XC-Sport (12793), 32/36 holes, 100 mm front and 130/135 mm rear. The printed p. 17 photograph shows both quick-release hubs alongside a separate freewheel. The p. 41 installation drawing depicts a rear hub with those spacings and a threaded freewheel seat. The 1987 Sansin dimensions recorded on 9007 are 100 mm front and 120/126 mm rear. The matching code stem, earlier Bianchi SunTour wording and matching front dimensions support checking the possible OEM relationship. Retain separate entries given the differing rear specifications and lack of a marked comparison. Targeted searches returned no directly coded HB-CE60 specimen or OEM key, and the 1987 Sansin scan host returned HTTP 503 during the comparison.

**Applied and validated with user approval.** Extended 9007 to 1987–92, updated source 234 to 563 and recorded the 1992 M400 fitment alongside the earlier specifications. Readback verified those fields and the five existing links (18456, 22252, 22362, 22486 and 22508). The comparison covered 24,829 specifications across 118 catalogues and matched the baseline, including the 15 pre-existing Huret losses. ### CE-60 flange and rear spacing follow-up — 2026-11-28

Recovered Sansin Catalog No. 11. The [back cover](https://cyclotourist.web.fc2.com/catarogue/part108/images/17.jpg) dates the printing to December 1987. Printed [p. 5](https://cyclotourist.web.fc2.com/catarogue/part108/images/05.jpg) gives the CE-60 loose balls and [p. 6](https://cyclotourist.web.fc2.com/catarogue/part108/images/06.jpg) gives the CE-60S 6001RS bearings. Both list 100 mm front and 120/126 mm rear over locknuts, with a BSC 1.37 × 24 TPI rear shell thread. The [p. 16 options table](https://cyclotourist.web.fc2.com/catarogue/part108/images/16.jpg) gives the spoke-hole pitch circle as 36 mm front on CE-60, 43 mm front on CE-60S and 46 mm rear on both. It marks a sealed system as an option for the loose-ball CE-60, so period bike specifications saying “sealed” alone cannot establish the S suffix.

[Sutherland's Handbook, sixth edition (1995)](https://paulinoposada.github.io/bike/documents/epdf.pub_sutherlands-handbook-for-bicycle-mechanics.pdf#page=247) measures flange diameter between spoke-hole centres, the same dimension Sansin calls P.C.D. The [front table](https://paulinoposada.github.io/bike/documents/epdf.pub_sutherlands-handbook-for-bicycle-mechanics.pdf#page=254) lists 39 mm and 33 mm centre to flange for HB-CE60, with 100 mm over locknuts. The [rear table](https://paulinoposada.github.io/bike/documents/epdf.pub_sutherlands-handbook-for-bicycle-mechanics.pdf#page=262) gives 45 mm and 31 mm left / 23 mm right centre to flange for the 135 mm seven-speed rear. The SunTour front differs from both Sansin front drillings. The rear locknut spacing is the clearer distinction, since the rear flange diameters differ by only 1 mm.

Keep 9007 and 12793 as distinct configurations. The [1993 SR SunTour catalogue, p. 10](https://www.mtb-kataloge.de/Bikekataloge/PDF/SunTour/SunTour93.pdf#page=10) pictures an uncoded XC-Sport pair. The [1994 manual, pp. 16–17](https://www.radiolabworks.com/bikes/catalogs/Suntour%201994%20%28SR%20Suntour%29%20Bicycle%20Equipment%20Sales%20Manual.pdf#page=18) provides the exact HB-CE60 codes. It lists a front HB-CE60 and compatibility with Joytech or Shimano rear freehubs under XC-Sport MD. The QR threaded HB-CE60 rear is pictured under standard XC-Sport. The earlier [Sansin XCD listing, p. 9](https://cyclotourist.web.fc2.com/catarogue/part108/images/09.jpg) documents a relationship for other hubs. The HB-CE60 OEM remains open pending a parts cross-reference or a maker-marked specimen with the HB-CE60 code.

Live readback found the same five named hub specs on 9007, including Brava 18456, and zero links on 12793. The Sansin hub near-duplicate report found zero pairs. This review made no database or mapping changes.
