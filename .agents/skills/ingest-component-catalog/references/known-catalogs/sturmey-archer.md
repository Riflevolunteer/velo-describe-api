# Sturmey-Archer catalogues

## 1971 trade catalogue and early hub records — reviewed 2026-11-23

The [Raleigh Industries trade scan](https://www.icenicam.org.uk/library/Sturmey-Archer/Catalogue_1971.pdf)
prints copyright 1971 on PDF p.1. Section III is Sturmey-Archer's manufacturer
range and service section for the British trade. PDF page numbers below are
one-based. The complete control assemblies have their own models or order
codes. Alternate cable lengths, clips and drilling for the same model belong
in the description. Internal repair parts, individual replacement sprockets,
cables, clips, lamps and battery units were excluded. PDF pp.1–6 are covers,
contents and introduction, 43 is blank, 54–55 list lighting accessories, 61
lists control spares, 62–77 are workshop data, and 78–80 are back matter.

### Earlier date and specification evidence

G. M. Beardshaw's [Hub Reference Book](https://www.sturmey-archerheritage.com/files/view-1396.pdf)
prints July 1984 on PDF p.3. It distinguishes the Hub Record's production
chronology, pp.6–17, from period retail availability. The production range
for FW is 1945–70 (p.12). The archived [FW series 6862 instructions](https://www.icenicam.org.uk/library/Sturmey-Archer/FW_hub_instructions_1946.pdf)
p.3 gives its early screw-on sprockets and ratios, and the 1947 Raleigh guide
p.2 still presents FW as a new option. The complete hub is listed in the
1973 US parts catalogue p.I–20, which documents trade availability after the
production bound. Beardshaw dates FG from 1947, ASC and FC from 1947, and
all three are in the [1955 Raleigh range](https://threespeedhub.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/Raleigh_Catalog_1955_UK.pdf).
Heritage indexes ASC and a maker advert to 1946, retained as the earlier
attestation. It indexes FC to 1946 too, but the known retail carton is dated
1947 by the archive and the production record gives 1947. Beardshaw's dates
for AC, FG, FM and SW end approximately in 1960. The
[Halfords catalogue indexed as 1960](https://www.icenicam.org.uk/library/Halford/Accessories_1960.pdf)
p.21 independently stocks AM, FM, FW and FG, and dates AM's introduction to
1937. Use Beardshaw's 1936–59 AM production range, with the alternate trade
dates preserved here. The [1 July 1938 Trader service article](https://www.icenicam.org.uk/library/Sturmey-Archer/AW_-_AM_-_AB_hub_service_sheet_1938-07-01.pdf)
p.iv confirms AM construction and AB's expanding brake. The [SW maker sheet](https://www.icenicam.org.uk/library/Sturmey-Archer/SW_hub_instructions_1954.pdf)
pp.1–3 supplies ratios, axle/sprocket range and pawl construction. Its 1954
date comes from the archive and independently agrees with Beardshaw's record.

The [1950s loose-leaf Master Catalogue](https://www.icenicam.org.uk/library/Sturmey-Archer/Hub_master_catalogue_1957-04-09.pdf)
has a 1959 price insert (PDF p.2), an August 1957 insert (p.4) and intentionally
separate current/discontinued sections (p.12). AW/AB/AG are in the latter in
that version, yet reappear in later maker material. PDF p.10 introduces SW.
Use the bind-in page dates and their purpose before drawing range conclusions.
The 1971 comparison settled the smooth AW model details. The Master Catalogue
index provides starting points for remaining earlier versions. Beardshaw lists
KS and KB families, but extra markings on 2730/2731 need a period legend or
matching specimens before assigning those
family dates. Existing earlier quadrant and brass trigger variants likewise
need code/finish evidence before changing their dates.

### TCW and SB hubs — reviewed 2026-11-23

The Master Catalogue's unmarked TCW sheet and parts list (PDF pp.36–37)
describe a three-speed Tricoaster hub with AW ratios (low −25%, direct,
high +33⅓%), 36/40-hole shells, 5¾/6¼-inch axles and 16–20 or 22-tooth
sprockets. The sheets are undated; a March 1959 insert lists TCW spares,
which does not establish availability of a complete hub at that date.
The [Raleigh US catalogue attributed to 1954](https://threespeedhub.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/Raleigh-Catalog-1954-US.pdf)
illustrates and offers a complete TCW; its year is the archive's attribution,
not a verified printed date.

Beardshaw's Hub Reference Book, PDF p.14, identifies three distinct TCW
constructions and their production ranges: Mark I, 1952–59, with one-piece
aluminium-bronze brake band and end-cam actuator; Mark II, 1959–61, with
bronze band and split steel liner and changed drag spring; and Mark III,
1961–72, with worm-drive brake actuator and wire drag spring. The
[heritage archive](https://www.sturmey-archerheritage.com/index.php?id=650&page=history-detail)
independently gives the Mark III dates. None of these three was in
the database before this review; the later S3C 2733 is a different model.
The approved additions are Mark I **12411**, Mark II **12412**, and Mark III
**12413**, all using existing source 522. Readback verified the fields and an
identical rerun affected zero rows. The brand near-duplicate report found zero
pairs. Generator comparison before and after was identical for 118 loaded
catalogues and 24,829 specs, with the same 15 unrelated lost-match proposals.
The whole-database quality report after application had zero errors.

The Master Catalogue's SB sheets (pp.34–35) describe a planned three-speed,
wide-ratio hub with expanding brake and cable or rod control. Its undated
errata (p.14) says AB and AG supplies would continue until SB and SG
production commenced. The [heritage archive](https://www.sturmey-archerheritage.com/index.php?id=945&page=history-detail)
states that SB was never produced. Do not add an SB production record on the
strength of the planned catalogue sheets.

### Applied and validated 2026-11-23

The approved transaction registered sources **521** (1971 trade catalogue) and
**522** (Beardshaw's July 1984 reference book), updated 18 existing components,
inserted 13 models, and added the Brake Levers / Sturmey Archer category entry
(category 2, brand 281). Exact readback verified all changed fields and the new
source records. An identical rerun affected zero rows. The whole-database
quality report remained at zero errors and 1,981 review findings. The brand
duplicate report found the existing AW case, trigger and stem distinctions
and zero near pairs. Original S5 12365 and later family entry 10020 represent
different catalogue eras and remain distinct.

No live bike spec referenced Sturmey-Archer or named Sturmey-Archer, Dynohub or
Tricoaster. Before and after generator reports were identical for 118 loaded
catalogues and 24,829 specs. The same 15 unrelated lost-match proposals remain.
No bike specs or generator overrides were edited.
